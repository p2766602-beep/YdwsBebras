 Bebras  India 
 Introduction 
 Bebras  (  www.bebras.org  )  is  an  international  student  Computational  Thinking  Challenge 
 organised  in  over  80  countries  and  designed  to  get  students  from  all  over  the  world  excited 
 about  computing.  The  challenge  is  a  great  way  to  learn  about  computational  thinking  and 
 problem solving skills. 
 The  Bebras  challenges  are  made  of  a  set  of  short  problems  called  Bebras  tasks.  The  tasks  are 
 fun,  engaging  and  based  on  problems  that  Computer  Scientists  enjoy  solving.  The  tasks 
 require  logical  thinking  and  can  be  solved  without  prior  knowledge  of  computational 
 thinking.  The aim is to solve as many as you can in the allotted time. 
 Bebras  India  Challenge  (  www.bebras.in  )  is  organized  by  ACM  India’s  CSpathshala  initiative 
 (  www.cspathshala.org  ).  ACM  India/CSpathshala’s  goal  is  to  make  it  possible  so  that  every 
 child in India learns Computer Science. 
 The  2024  Bebras  India  Challenge  was  held  from  17th  November  to  7th  December,  2024.  The 
 2024  Bebras  India  Computational  Thinking  Challenge  was  oﬀered  for  age  groups  8-19  in  12 
 languages:  English,  Marathi,  Gujarati,  Hindi,  Bangla,  Bodo,  Punjabi,  Kannada,  Odia,  Tamil, 
 Telugu and Assamese and is conducted for free. 
 For  the  very  ﬁrst  time  the  challenge  was  oﬀered  to  ﬁrst  year  undergraduate  students  (ages 
 17-19)  in  English.  The  2024  Bebras  India  Challenge  We  had  participation  of  2,69,931 
 students from 22 states and 2 UT in 12 languages in age groups 8-19.

 Lost 
 Anita was playing in the forest. But when it was time to go home, she forgot which path to 
 take. She remembered that on her way through the forest: 
 She ﬁrst saw red and yellow ﬂowers. 
 Then, she saw an apple tree. 
 Finally, she spotted a bird's nest in a tree. 
 The picture below shows where Anita got lost along with paths leading out of the forest. 
 Question: 
 Which path should Anita choose to go home? 
 A.  B.  C.  D. 
 January 2025  Page |  7 

===== PAGE 9 =====
 Bebras  India 
 Answer Explanation: 
 The correct answer is B. 
 On the way to the forest, Alia ﬁrst saw red and yellow ﬂowers; second, an apple tree; and 
 third, a bird nest on a tree. On the way home, she sees the same objects, but in the opposite 
 order. So, on the way home, she sees a bird nest ﬁrst, then an apple tree, and last the red 
 and yellow ﬂowers. Hence, B is the correct answer. 
 Path A is incorrect, because there are no bird nests in the trees. 
 Path C is incorrect because the order of the ﬂowers, apple tree and birds nest is reversed. 
 Path D is incorrect because there are no red and yellow ﬂowers, but the blue one instead. 
 It’s Informatics: 
 This problem simulates a stack data structure that uses the LIFO (Last In First Out) process. 
 The objects seen by Alia can be thought of as objects put into a stack. The ﬁrst object seen 
 (red and yellow ﬂowers) is put into a stack, followed by the second object (apple tree) and 
 the third object (bird nest on a tree) so that the stack can be described as follows: 
 When Alia walks in the opposite direction, each object in the stack needs to be removed. 
 The ﬁrst thing Alia sees on her way home is a bird nest on a tree. Then the bird nest is 
 removed from the stack so that in the top position is the apple tree. Then the apple tree is 
 removed from the stack as well. The last item remaining from the stack was the red and 
 yellow tree. 
 This is Computational Thinking 
 To solve this task, we need to focus on certain things and ignore others. We only need to pay 
 attention to the red and yellow ﬂowers, the apple tree, and the bird nest. Everything else 
 doesn't help us solve the problem, so we can forget about them. This is called abstraction.

 It’s Informatics: 
 Often, when dealing with computer science problems, you have a large amount of data, but 
 only a small part of it is relevant to a particular question. For this task, too, we only need

 Ovi shakes the rattle one more time.

 Which picture shows Ovi’s rattle? 
 A.  B. 
 C.  D. 
 January 2025  Page |  15 

===== PAGE 17 =====
 Bebras  India 
 Answer Explanation: 
 D. is the correct answer 
 When balls go through the rattle, they cannot change their order. So you only need to check 
 the order of all balls.  After the red ball  comes  the yellow one  . After the green one 
 is the blue one  . Then comes the purple , then  comes red  again. 
 It’s Informatics: 
 Computer scientists represent and store the data in diﬀerent ways. All data representations 
 have advantages that depend on the properties of the respective data. 
 In this task a so-called circular linked list is used and is called a “rattle” and the stored 
 elements are the balls. In this circular-linked list, each element has a so-called pointer to the 
 next element. Also, the pointer of the last element points back to the ﬁrst element and thus 
 closes the circle. 
 That means, in this task, we know for each ball which ball is next. After the last ball is the 
 ﬁrst one. When we check the order of the balls, we can start from any ball and need to go 
 through all of them (the whole list) in the correct order. 
 There are two advantages when we use a circular linked list to store elements. The ﬁrst 
 advantage is that in a circular linked list there is no endpoint. Since the list loops back to the 
 beginning the elements never run out of data to check or use. The second advantage is the 
 eﬃciency of the navigation through the list. It is easy to move through the list without 
 having to follow special rules for the end point. You just keep going in one direction. 
 This is Computational Thinking 
 In this task, you tried to look for a pattern. This is called pattern recognition. Recognising 
 patterns is helpful to determine if some problems are similar or even the same. Then, we 
 don’t need to solve each of them separately, instead we can use the solution to one of them 
 to help us solve the other.

 Unlike  a  traditional  queue,  we  can  add  an  element  to  either  end  of  a  deque  and  remove  an 
 element from either end of the deque. A deque is a data structure. 
 Deques  have  many  uses,  for  example  as  a  generalised  replacement  for  both  stacks  (add  at 
 the  front  and  remove  at  the  front)  and  simple  queues  (add  at  the  back  and  remove  at  the 
 front).  You  can  think  of  it  like  a  shunting  track:  wagons  can  be  added  or  removed  at  the  front 
 and  back,  but  you  cannot  simply  remove  a  wagon  from  the  middle.  On  a  simple  shunting 
 track,  on  the  other  hand,  you  could  only  add  or  remove  wagons  on  one  side  (like  a  stack) 
 and on a hump, the wagons only ever move in one direction (like a queue). 
 Just  as  the  tube  of  this  task  can  only  hold  up  to  three  balls,  a  deque  also  has  a  limited 
 capacity  in  practice.  If  an  element  is  added  to  a  deque  that  is  already  at  capacity,  space  must 
 be  made  at  the  other  end.  This  is  done  by  removing  an  element  from  there  and  losing  the 
 corresponding information. 
 Storage space is a ﬁnite resource and the amount of data you can store depends on it. 
 Nevertheless, deques are often used as hypothetically unlimited, especially when dealing 
 with theoretical questions rather than practical applications.

 Gathering gifts 
 Anita bought seven gifts for her brothers, Amu and Bablu. There are 3 gifts for Amu, and 4 
 gifts for Bablu. Anita placed the gifts and cards in a shelf in compartments as shown below, 
 and will give Amu and Bablu each a card with the compartment number to start with. 
 Amu’s card says 6, so in order to get his gifts, Amu will start by opening the gift in 
 compartment 6. Next he will go to the compartment indicated on the gift and open that gift, 
 and keep going until he ﬁnds the “END” card. By following the “chain” of cards, Amu will 
 eventually receive all his gifts. 
 Question: 
 Which compartment number did Bablu start with to get all his gifts? 
 A)  1  B)  3  C)  5  D)   7 
 January 2025  Page |  26 

===== PAGE 28 =====
 Bebras  India 
 The correct answer is: B) 3 
 Answer Explanation: 
 The correct answer is: Amu: 6, 2, 4. Bablu: 3, 1, 5, 7. 
 Amu starts with the gift in compartment 6, then is directed to compartment 2, and then to 
 compartment 4, where he receives the “END” card. 
 To ﬁnd Bablu’s compartments, we need to work backwards. The last compartment will be 
 compartment 7, because it contains the other “END” card. Since compartment 5 leads to 
 compartment 7, then Bablu’s third compartment must be 5. Since compartment 1 leads to 
 compartment 5, then Bablu’s second compartment must be 1. Since compartment 3 leads to 
 compartment 1, then Bablu’s ﬁrst compartment must be 3. So Bablu’s compartments in 
 order from start to ﬁnish are 3, 1, 5, 7. 
 It’s important to note that compartments 6 and 3 are the only numbers not written on any 
 cards in the compartments, so this suggests they are the two compartments that Amu and 
 Bablu start with. 
 It’s Informatics: 
 In  informatics,  data  structures  similar  to  the  “chains”  of  gifts  are  called  (simply-)linked  lists. 
 As  with  any  other  piece  of  data  in  a  computer’s  memory,  the  information  that  provides 
 access  to  a  list  is  an  address,  precisely  that  of  the  ﬁrst  element  (in  the  case  of  the  task,  the

 It’s Informatics: 
 The  task  shows  how  a  scheduler  works.  A  scheduler  is  a  program  that  belongs  to  the 
 operating  system  of  a  computer.  While  a  computer  is  running,  many  tasks  must  be 
 processed. 
 For  each  task,  a  process  is  started  that  solves  the  task.  The  Scheduler  controls  the  ﬂow  of 
 processes.  It  determines  when  and  for  how  long  a  process  is  executed  by  the  computer's 
 central processing unit (CPU) 
 There are diﬀerent strategies that schedulers use: 
 ●  Priority  Scheduling  –  The  process  with  the  highest  priority  is  executed  next  (fully). 
 This procedure is used in the task. 
 ●  First  Come  First  Serve  (FCFS)  –  The  process  that  is  ﬁrst  in  the  queue  is  processed  ﬁrst. 
 The  person  who  has  come  is  the  ﬁrst  to  be  served  (completely).  This  is  the  principle 
 that most queues in everyday life work according to (“ﬁrst come, ﬁrst served”). 
 ●  Round  Robin  –  the  waiting  processes  are  processed  in  turn  only  for  a  certain  time 
 executed.  If  a  process  is  not  ﬁnished,  it  is  interrupted  and  has  to  go  back  to  the  end 
 of the queue. Then it's the next process's turn. 
 ●  Shortest  Job  First  (SJF)  –  The  process  with  the  shortest  expected  work  time  is 
 executed  ﬁrst  (completely).  In  this  task,  the  Shortest  Job  First  strategy  was  used 
 because  the  beaver  with  the  fewest  books  (which  could  be  processed  the  fastest) 
 was served next.
